What we actually checked

Rather than take the plan at face value, we went through the provider documentation line by line, because two of the assumptions in it turn out to work differently than expected — and one of them is the kind of mistake you only find out about after the number has stopped working.

  • Aircall's port requirements for the UK specifically — which number types they accept, what paperwork the carrier demands, and how long it really takes.
  • What WhatsApp is actually tied to: the SIM, the handset, or the number.
  • Meta's current route for bringing an existing WhatsApp Business number into an API without deleting the account — and what it costs you in features.

The QR code is the constraint that sets the whole design

You said the QR code is why you can't afford any issues with the WhatsApp number. You're right, and we can now be specific about why.

Finding · scan for a free quote

The number is printed inside the code

The QR on the van is too low-resolution in the photo you sent for us to decode it outright — but it's a dense code, which means a long URL rather than a short domain. And the WhatsApp button on your own website points here:

wa.me/447440517077?text=Hi%20C1%20Clearance…&utm_source=website

If the van's QR is the same style of link — and the density says it is — then the mobile number is physically printed on the vehicle, just encoded rather than legible. Change the number and the QR points at a dead account. That's a re-wrap, not a settings change.

Action before anything else

Scan the actual van with a phone and screenshot where it lands. Two minutes, and it settles the single biggest unknown in this project. If it resolves to a wa.me/447440517077 link, the recommendation below becomes non-negotiable rather than merely sensible.

Fix this permanently on the next print run

Point future QR codes at a short link on your own domain — c1clearance.co.uk/q — which then redirects to WhatsApp. The printed code never has to change again, you can repoint it to a different number or channel whenever you like, and you get scan counts per vehicle. Right now every scan is invisible to you; you can't tell the van from Google. That's a fortnight of work for a permanent piece of flexibility, and it's reusable for every signwritten client stato.tech takes on.

04

Three corrections to the original plan

The instinct — get everything into one place, use Aircall because it records and integrates cleanly — is right. Three of the mechanics need changing.

Original plan: transfer the WhatsApp number from Lycamobile to Aircall.

WhatsApp is tied to the number, not the SIM — and that cuts both ways

You don't need to move the number to get WhatsApp off a phone in a van. WhatsApp lives against the number, and Meta will let that number run on their API while it stays on its existing SIM. Porting it into Aircall doesn't help you and introduces a real hazard: the number stops living on a mobile network, so the next time WhatsApp asks to re-verify it — new handset, wiped phone, lost device, account check — you're depending on a verification code reaching a VoIP line. If that fails, the number printed inside the QR code on your van no longer has WhatsApp on it.

Instead: leave 07440 517077 on a SIM and bring it into the lead centre through Meta's Coexistence route. Same number, same chat history, staff can still answer on the handset, and the QR code never notices anything happened. If the reason for the move was getting off Lycamobile, port the number to a proper business mobile plan instead — that keeps it on a real network where verification is reliable.

Original plan: forward the number to Aircall while the 21-day port completes.

A divert carries calls, not WhatsApp

Call forwarding is a voice-network feature. WhatsApp traffic doesn't ride on it — messages keep arriving at whatever device holds that WhatsApp account, regardless of where the calls go. So the divert is a fine bridge for the office line, but it does nothing for the actual problem you're trying to solve, which is WhatsApp enquiries being stuck on one handset.

Instead: treat the two as separate jobs. Voice gets diverted on day one. WhatsApp gets joined via Coexistence, which takes hours, not weeks — and is the part that immediately stops leads getting lost.

Original plan: 21 days to port.

Aircall's own documentation says up to six weeks for the UK

UK ports also need paperwork that trips people up: a Letter of Authorisation, a provider invoice from the last 30 days, the CUPID code or current carrier name, and — critically — the Main Billing Number confirmed with your existing provider. If the number you're porting is the main billing number, everything billed under it has to move or be disconnected at the same time. Mobile ports need a PAC as well, which you get by texting PAC to 65075 and which expires after 30 days.

Instead: plan for four to six weeks and don't let the timeline matter. The divert means recording, routing and logging are live in week one; the port is just tidying up the plumbing behind it.

05

What we'd build

Five inputs, one lead record. The lead centre in stato.tech owns the customer history; Aircall and WhatsApp are just pipes into it.

The bit that matters for a clearance business specifically

Clearance quoting runs on photographs. Someone points a phone at a garage full of stuff, sends four pictures, and wants a price. A generic CRM treats those as attachments and buries them. The lead centre should treat them as the primary content of the lead: photos on the record, on the quote, and on the job sheet the crew sees, with the postcode, access notes and volume estimate pulled out of the conversation and pinned to the top. That is the difference between a shared inbox and something that actually shortens the time from scan to quote.

Reusable across stato.tech

Almost none of this is C1-specific. Channel adapters, identity matching on phone numbers, the unified timeline, the pipeline engine, source attribution, SLA alerting — these are the module. C1 is tenant one, and the next trades client is a configuration exercise rather than a rebuild. That's the argument for building it properly now.

Sequence

Deliberately ordered so the risky, irreversible steps happen last and the visible wins happen first.

Days 1–3Phase 0

Establish the facts

  • Scan the van QR and record exactly where it goes.
  • Pull the Lycamobile account details — registered name and address must match the port paperwork exactly, since mismatches are the main cause of rejection.
  • Confirm who holds 0330 133 9144 and whether it's the main billing number on that account.
  • Check the WhatsApp Business app version, fix the profile photo and details, back up the chat history.
Week 1Phase 1

Calls recorded and logged

Aircall account, a temporary number, IVR and routing set up, and a divert from the office line onto it. Recording, voicemail and call logging go live immediately — no waiting on any port. Aircall webhooks wired into a staging lead centre so calls start creating records.

Weeks 1–2Phase 2

WhatsApp into the lead centre

Coexistence onboarding on 07440 517077 through a BSP, then Cloud API webhooks into stato.tech. Chat history preserved, handset still works, QR code untouched. This is the phase that stops leads being lost, and it's the fastest one.

Weeks 2–6Phase 3

Port the office line

Submit the port for 0330 133 9144 with the LOA, a recent invoice and carrier details. The divert stays in place throughout, so the cutover is invisible to callers. Nothing is cancelled with the existing provider until the port completes.

Weeks 3–6Phase 4

The rest of the funnel, and the actual product

Email, website form and Meta lead ads into the same record. Then the parts that earn their keep: pipeline stages built around how clearance jobs really run, photo-led quoting, response-time alerts, and source attribution so you finally know whether the van, Google or Meta is paying for itself.

LaterPhase 5

Automation, once the data is clean

Call summaries, automatic extraction of postcode, access and volume from a conversation, first-response auto-replies out of hours. Worth doing after the foundation is live, not before — automation on top of messy data just produces confident nonsense faster.

Time-sensitive · six weeks away

Meta is changing this on 1 October 2026. Free-form replies inside the 24-hour customer service window have been free since November 2024 — from October they're charged per message at the same rate as utility templates, and in-window utility templates lose their free status too. Exact UK rates are due from Meta by 1 September.

Two consequences worth designing around. First, budget for it: at 400 enquiries and eight outbound replies each, that's roughly £50 a month rather than nothing. Second, messages sent from the WhatsApp Business app itself stay free — which is another quiet argument for Coexistence over a full API migration, because you keep a free channel for long back-and-forth threads while the API handles logging, automation and reporting. Conversations that start from a click-to-WhatsApp ad also keep a free 72-hour window, which makes ad-driven enquiries meaningfully cheaper than QR scans. That's a media-planning insight you can act on.

10

Compliance, briefly

Not legal advice, and worth a short conversation with whoever handles C1's data protection — but these are the four things this build touches.

  • Call recording. Callers need to be told at the start of the call, there needs to be a lawful basis for it, and a retention period that's actually enforced. Aircall keeps recordings for a year on the entry plan and indefinitely higher up — indefinitely is a liability, not a feature, so set a deletion rule.
  • WhatsApp marketing. Replying to an enquiry is fine. Sending marketing templates to a list needs opt-in, and Meta polices this through message quality ratings independently of UK law.
  • Photos of people's homes. Interior photographs of a property, tied to an address and a name, are personal data with a slightly awkward flavour. Retention limits, sensible access controls, and don't keep them past the invoice.
  • Chat history. Migrating history into the lead centre widens who can see past conversations. Worth a line in the privacy policy and a role-based access model rather than everyone seeing everything.
